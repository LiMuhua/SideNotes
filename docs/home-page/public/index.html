SideNotes

The notebook hidden at the edge of your screen.

Push the pointer against the edge of your screen, or press Alt + Shift + S, and SideNotes slides out. Jot down a to-do, a command or a link. Move the pointer away and it slides back, leaving your work just as it was.

Catch a thought from anywhere

Press Ctrl + Shift + Space and a small capture window opens over whatever you're doing. Type, press Ctrl + Enter, and the note is waiting in the sidebar.

When a line turns into a page, carry on in the sidebar with all your other notes around it.

Small notes, kept in order

  • Markdown, one shortcut away

    Headings, lists, to-dos, code blocks and links all have shortcuts. Paste a screenshot or drop a file straight into a note.

  • Tags and tabs

    Write a #tag anywhere in a note. Pin a note to keep it on top, and turn the tags you use most into tabs.

  • Search every note

    Ctrl + F looks through all your notes. Words match titles, text and attachment names; #tags match tags. Results are grouped by tab.

  • Notes in their own windows

    Drag a note out of the sidebar to open it in a window. Keep it on top of other apps, or merge several windows into tabs.

  • Out of your way

    The sidebar slides away when the pointer leaves it. Pin it when you want it to stay, and dock it to the left or right edge.

  • Shortcuts you choose

    Rebind any shortcut in Settings. If another app already uses a key, SideNotes tells you and keeps the old one.

Five themes, each in light and dark

SideNotes follows your system's light or dark mode, or stays in the one you pick. The font, text size, sidebar width and app icon are yours to change too.

Your notes stay on your computer

  • No account, no sign-up

    Notes, attachments and settings are kept in a local database on your computer. Nothing leaves it unless you turn on cloud sync.

  • A snapshot every day

    SideNotes takes a snapshot of your notes every day and before steps like importing a backup, and can restore any of them.

  • Cloud sync, only if you want it

    Cloud sync stays off until you set it up. Then SideNotes saves snapshots to storage you own, a Synology NAS over WebDAV, OneDrive or Google Drive, on a schedule or whenever you sync.

  • Nobody in between

    SideNotes has no servers and collects no analytics. Your data goes straight from your computer to the service you connect.

How SideNotes uses Google user data

SideNotes accesses Google Drive only if you choose Google Drive for cloud sync and sign in with your Google account. It requests a single scope, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file, which gives it access only to the files and folders that SideNotes itself creates in your Google Drive.

What SideNotes does with this access

  • Creates the folder you choose for your snapshots, and uploads snapshots of your notes and attachments into it when you sync.
  • Lists and downloads those snapshots when you restore your notes.
  • Deletes old snapshots from that folder according to the retention rule.
  • Reads your Google account's email address, to show in Settings which account is connected.

What it doesn't do

  • It can't see, read or change any other files in your Google Drive.
  • It has no servers: data travels directly between your computer and Google.
  • It doesn't sell or share Google user data, use it for advertising or to train AI models, and no person reads it.

Signing out in Settings deletes the stored sign-in token and revokes SideNotes's access with Google. You can also remove SideNotes from your Google Account's third-party connections at any time.

SideNotes's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Read the full Privacy Policy.
